02 What your own search misses The market you hire from is a choice

No company sees the whole market.

Every company hires from the part of the market it knows how to reach: its city, its job boards, the agency someone used before, the salary band set two years ago. That boundary was never decided. It accumulated.

For most roles it does not matter. For a hard one it decides the outcome, because the person who could do the job is often just outside it. Moving that boundary is the whole of what we do.

04 What we actually do Research · find · interview · introduce

We recruit outside the market you already search.

A recruiter paid per role in one city cannot afford to build a presence in another. We are set up the other way round: standing outside our clients' markets is a condition of how we operate, not extra effort we promise.

01

Research

We find out where the people who could do your role actually are, and count them. You see that map before you commit to anything, and it states what we could not measure.

02

Find and approach

We contact those people ourselves. Not a bought list and not applicants: people located for this role, in markets a local agency cannot afford to work.

03

Interview and introduce

We interview them against your standard, in the language the role needs, and introduce the ones who clear it, with the list of who we ruled out and why.

The concentration, drawn

Filled: one client. Outlined: the other seven companies, five of which appear exactly once.

What this proves

One company, over three years, repeatedly chose to hire people it could not reach on its own, across nine unrelated functions, through the same desk. Breadth of function and persistence over time are the two things hardest to manufacture.

What it doesn't

That it repeats. The table records wins only, so nothing here supports a conversion, fill or win rate, and we will not quote one. We hold no verified figure for placement value. One record is a fall-off.
