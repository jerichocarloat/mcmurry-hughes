Evidence

The market is bigger than the shortlist.

A market map is the research we do for one role: a count of the people who could do that job outside the market the client already searches.

It is not a candidate database, a list of names, or a map of the world. It is one role, counted and dated, with the parts we could not measure stated on the same page. Below is a live one, built from illustrative data so you can use it.

What a marker says

The role they do now, years of experience, where they are, their language, what we know and what we don't. Every one ends with not contacted, because none of them have been. Nobody here has applied to anything or said they are available.

What it never says

A name, a photograph, a current employer or a link. The map is evidence that a market exists outside your search. It is not a list of people you can buy.

What the grey block means

The market the client already searches. In this example, Stuttgart. Grey squares are people inside it. Vermilion squares are people outside it, who that search would never reach.

What this proves

One company, over three years, kept choosing to hire people its own recruiting could not reach, across nine unrelated kinds of job, through the same desk. Breadth of function and persistence over time are the two things hardest to manufacture.

What it doesn't

That it repeats with anyone else. The table records placements only, never the companies we approached and did not win, so nothing here supports a conversion, fill or win rate, and we will not quote one. We hold no verified figure for what a placement is worth. One record is a fall-off. Four rows have dates that need cleaning before anyone uses them for anything.

Why the catch is on the page

Most recruiting sites put the best number in the biggest type and hide the catch. Ours puts both at the same size, on the same screen. A buyer who finds it on their own has caught us out. A buyer who is told it is watching a company that counts carefully.
